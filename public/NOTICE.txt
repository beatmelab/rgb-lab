CIE reference and derived numerical data
----------------------------------------
International Commission on Illumination (CIE):
- CIE 1931 standard colorimetric observer (2019):
  https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.xvudnb9b
- CIE standard illuminant D65 (2019):
  https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.hjfjmt59
- Spectral radiance factors of 99 colour-fidelity test samples (2017):
  https://doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.wi5idbqu

Original data and CIE-derived numerical arrays: Creative Commons
Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0):
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

Changes made for RGB Lab: the observer is normalized to xy for the spectral
boundary (380–780 nm, 1 nm); surface calculations use a 5 nm observer and
Y-normalized D65; five colour-sample columns are selected unchanged. Synthetic
LED spectra are fitted using the observer to match the nominal emitter xyY.
These are illustrative models, not CIE-approved fixture calibrations. The
original datasets and metadata, selected samples, checksums, and generation
procedure are documented in the repository's src/data/README.md. No endorsement
by CIE is implied. The CIE-derived data remains licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.

NIST skin reflectance
---------------------
Catherine C. Cooksey, David W. Allen and Benjamin K. Tsai (2017),
Reference Data Set of Human Skin Reflectance, Journal of Research of NIST 122.
Article: https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.122.026
Data: https://doi.org/10.18434/M38597
Source: https://opendata.nist.gov/1832_Data_JResNIST_skinrefl%20v3.txt
NIST terms: https://www.nist.gov/director/licensing

Attributed US government data. Subjects 44, 70 and 22 (Average curves) were
selected as the lowest D65 L*, the nearest full-range perceptual midpoint,
and the greatest D65 a* among samples within 3 L* of the brightest sample, then
linearly resampled from 3 nm to 5 nm. They are displayed as Dark, Medium and
Light on illustrative matte geometry, not photographs or measured facial maps.
These examples cover a broad range, not the full diversity of skin. No demographic
identity or NIST endorsement is implied.
